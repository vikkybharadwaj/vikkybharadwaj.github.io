Safe to Spend — Version History

Markdown companion: docs/FEATURE_VERSIONS_SAFE_TO_SPEND.md

Twenty iterations from a single hero number to a behaviorally-tuned runway with savings deliberately set aside, drill-downs on every aggregate, and per-tab projections. Each version shipped on real user data, each change was earned.

→ Safe to Spend math explorer — click-to-expand component breakdown of the whole formula → Typical Spending Forecast spec — day-by-day worked example

Thematic arc

Three big shifts across five weeks of iteration:

  • Information density up — one number (v0) → three timeframes (v1) → four tabs with math breakdown (v4) → full runway decomposition accordion (v4.3)
  • Data model consolidation — duplicated math across Home + AI Coach (v1) → shared safe_to_spend_service.ts (v3.1) → DB as single source of truth (v4.4)
  • Let the user edit reality when the detector gets it wrong — silent detection (v0–v4.3) → “Confirm your paycheck” (v4.5)
v0
2026-03-24
FOUNDATION
Safe-to-Spend hero card on the dashboard

What shipped

  • A single number: current account balance shown as “safe to spend”
  • Static balance, no payday awareness, no bills subtracted
  • Lived inside the broader dashboard-with-real-API-data effort

Why this was the starting point

Minimum viable affordance: give the user one honest number they can glance at. Most cashflow apps stop here. Quickly became clear that “your balance” is not the same as “what you can safely spend” — pending bills and upcoming rent change the answer dramatically.

CORRECTION
AI Coach daily budget matches Home

Why

AI Coach was computing its own daily budget; Home was computing a different one. Users asked the Coach “what can I spend today?” and got a different number than the hero card on Home. Hot-patched the AI Coach to match.

REWORK
Credit card debt in the formula

What changed

  • Formula now: spendableBalance − upcomingBills − creditCardDebt + expectedIncome

Why

Users with rolling CC balances saw “Safe to Spend = $X” when they actually owed $X+ to their cards and were negative in net worth. Subtracting CC debt made the number honest at the cost of looking smaller — a deliberate tradeoff for financial accuracy over optimism.

2026-04-19
REVERTED
Removed user-configured payday — and put it right back

What we tried

Attempted to remove user-facing payday configuration entirely and rely only on stream detection. The thinking: “we can tell when you get paid from your bank data — why ask?”

Why it was reverted

For new users with thin Plaid history, stream detection needs weeks of data before it’s reliable. Until then, payday predictions were wrong — and “wrong by design” for new users is a dealbreaker. User control had to stay.

v4.0
2026-04-20
REWORK
4 always-visible tabs, ISO week, 0.75 income threshold

What changed

  • All 4 tabs always rendered — no auto-hide based on redundancy (same number across tabs is fine)
  • Week became ISO Mon-Sun in the user’s timezone (not rolling 7 days)
  • Income math-inclusion threshold lowered from 0.85 to 0.75 — scoped ONLY to SafeToSpendService; Smart Pulse / Morning Brief narratives kept the stricter 0.85 bar
  • Per-window math added: daysInWindow + perDayInWindow computed server-side
  • Color legend with “seen” state via SecureStore
  • Breakdown shows total then derivation line: "= $perDayInWindow/day × daysInWindow days"
  • Removed the min(payCycle, dailyBudget×7) cap on This Week

What we learned

“If you want intuitive math, don’t lie about the cap.” Showing the honest per-window sum with a legible derivation beat a capped-but-opaque number. Consistency across tabs — even when redundant — felt more trustworthy than hidden logic.

v4.1
2026-04-22
CORRECTION
Per-tab amount = dailyBudget × daysInWindow, capped at runway

What changed

  • Per-tab amount formula: min(dailyBudget × daysInWindow, untilPayday.amount) for Today / This Week / This Month
  • Breakdown components (bills / income in window) preserved as CONTEXT but no longer arithmetically sum to amount

Why

On-device test revealed the v4 formula collapsed amount to spendable − ccDebt whenever no bills/income fell inside the window. Wednesday-to-Sunday “This Week” returned the same number as Until Payday. User’s mental model: “$777/day × 5 days left in week = $3,887.” Fixed by switching to the daily-budget-allocation model.

Lesson

“Mathematically honest” lost to “user-intuitive” on this one. Pay-cycle-daily-budget allocation > window-scoped sum.

2026-04-22
CORRECTION
Drop the cap, uniform $/day across all tabs

What changed

  • Removed the min(..., untilPayday.amount) cap from v4.1
  • All 4 tabs now show amount = dailyBudget × daysInWindow with uniform perDayInWindow = dailyBudget
  • When This Month extends past next payday, a footnote explains: “Extends N days past your next payday — this projection assumes the same daily rate continues.”
  • Breakdown UX reworked: every tab now shows how we derived the daily budget (runway ÷ days = $/day), then the per-tab math ($/day × days)
  • Components moved to a secondary “Also coming up in this window” section — context, not arithmetic inputs

Why

v4.1’s cap had a side effect: perDayInWindow = amount / daysInWindow varied across tabs. User feedback: “This Month is taking $777/day for 9 days whereas Until Payday is taking $874/day times 8 days — doesn’t make sense.” Dropping the cap preserved a single daily rate everywhere.

SIMPLIFY
Payday v2: 2 cases, DB single source of truth, date hydration fix

What changed

  • Payday precedence collapsed from 5 cases to 2: user_setting or inferred
  • DB profiles row is the ONLY source of truth — mobile writes via PUT /users/me/profile, no query-param overrides on SafeToSpend
  • Deleted: PaydayDivergenceBanner, isStale, divergenceWarning, legacyPaydaySource, thisMonth alias, legacy_data AI tool block, backfill ace command, “Last Paycheck” Settings row

Lesson

Complexity compounds silently. Every “edge case” branch added pre-PMF is a bug factory. Solve for the W2-salaried regular-paycheck user first; defer multiple income sources, job changes, thin Plaid history, and divergence detection.

Scope of the delete

Net −500+ LoC across backend + mobile. Zero eval fixture churn (distinguished-engineer review confirmed no fixture referenced any dropped field).

v4.5
2026-04-23
EVOLVE
Confirm your paycheck + semi-monthly variant + split confidence bars

What changed

  • “Confirm your paycheck” Settings row — user taps a specific income transaction, sets user_confirmed=true on its recurring_streams row
  • Split the confidence bar by decision: user_confirmed bypasses the ≥0.6 ANCHOR bar (dates trusted), but NOT the ≥0.75 AMOUNT bar (one observation isn’t a forecast)
  • Semi-monthly variant picker: “1st & 15th” vs “15th & Last day” — reuses existing payday_day_of_month column (1 or 15)
  • Normalizer extended to strip ACH metadata tags and mixed-alphanumeric transaction codes; 4-word cap on merchant keys
  • Bills deactivate-pass excludes user-confirmed streams so detect-and-replace cycle doesn’t wipe them
  • New endpoints: GET /paycheck-confirmations/candidates + POST /paycheck-confirmations

User trigger

Real-data reveal: the single confidence_score was gating TWO independent questions — “are the DATES reliable?” and “are the AMOUNTS reliable?” — and blended-low blocked the reliable-dates half. User: recent job change (1 paycheck, partial period) + paternity leave ending ($1,278–$3,197 variance). Both paths failed → 14-day fallback → negative runway on every tab. “Resolve it for me like I’m any other customer.”

Lesson

When auto-detection is uncertain, let the user tap ground truth. Recognition tasks (pick from a list) reliably beat recall tasks (remember a date). Confidence scores are a signal, not a verdict — one user tap beats six noisy observations.

v4.6
2026-04-23
EVOLVE
Drill-downs on every aggregated row + typical discretionary spending as CONTEXT

What changed

  • Aggregated accordion rows are now tap-to-expand: Checking → per-account balances, Bills before payday → per-merchant list with dates, Credit card balances → per-card balances. Single-row-open accordion (tapping a new row closes the previous) caps card height.
  • New "Projection at payday" block on the Until Payday tab, placed AFTER the per-tab math (NOT inserted between Runway and Daily Budget, which would break the arithmetic identity). Shows typical non-recurring spending and = if habits repeat, at payday.
  • New transactions.recurring_stream_id FK, populated by the recurrence detector. Lets downstream services filter recurring vs discretionary via WHERE recurring_stream_id IS NULL — no more error-prone normalized-name lookups.
  • New typical_discretionary_service.ts: up to 365 days of history, robust-mean-per-weekday capped at the 90th percentile, excludes non-discretionary primaries (INCOME, TRANSFER_IN/OUT, LOAN_PAYMENTS, BANK_FEES) and the CC-account side of CC bill payments. Adaptive lookback: min(365, daysOfHistoryAvailable), null when <60 days.
  • Methodology modal (tap the "How?" link on the projection header): sample size, observed-days coverage percentage, per-weekday bar chart, window dates. Honest about scope: day-of-week seasonality only; month-of-year needs multi-year data.
  • AI Coach's calculate_safe_to_spend tool now surfaces typicalDiscretionary so the model can answer "what if I go out this weekend?" with a grounded baseline.

User trigger

Vik noticed two gaps. (1) "If I want to drill down into what those specifically are, I think I need a way to know that" — Credit card balances $7,021 and Bills before payday $14 were opaque; the data was on the response but not surfaced. (2) "What if you are buying things on your debit card that's not recurring? Do you think we have actually taken into account that at all?" — future impulse spending isn't in the runway formula. Industry norm says don't try to predict it; Vik said close the gap as honest context, using "at least one year of data to predict seasonality."

What the formula captures now

Category Captured? How
Past debit / checking spending (cleared)✓spendableBalance reads availableBalance from Plaid
Past debit / checking spending (pending)✓Plaid availableBalance subtracts pending debits automatically
Past credit-card spending✓creditCardDebt = sum of current CC balances
Future recurring bills (rent, Netflix, gym)✓upcomingBills from recurring_streams (conf ≥ 0.3)
Future recurring income (paycheck)✓expectedIncome from income streams (conf ≥ 0.75)
Future non-recurring discretionaryContext (v4.6)typicalDiscretionary forecast, shown as “Projection at payday” — NOT subtracted from runway

Lesson

When one threshold is doing two jobs, split it. v4.5 split confidence into anchor-reliability vs amount-reliability. v4.6 splits recurrence-match into "exclude from discretionary forecast" (FK-based, precise) vs "stream-level membership" (name-based, lenient). Both times the split produced a better answer. Also: typical spend stays as CONTEXT, not a formula input — runway is the user's contract with the number; don't bury a prediction inside it.

EVOLVE
Horizontal-scroll drill-downs + CC swipes in the forecast + per-tab projections

What changed

  • Aggregated rows (Checking / Bills / CC balances / Typical spending) now carry a chevron affordance and reveal a horizontal-scrolling strip of compact cards when tapped. Previous inline-list drill-down felt visually weak and the tappability wasn't obvious.
  • typicalDiscretionary now spans both checking and credit card outflows. A future $50 Uber on CC is still a real outflow — excluding CC swipes was conservative but dishonest. User 53's sample size jumped 16 → 160 transactions (10×) just by lifting the depository-only filter.
  • Projection block appears on every tab (Today / This Week / This Month / Until Payday), not just Until Payday. Backend returns a typicalDiscretionary.windows map; each tab reads its own window's forecastAmount. perDayOfWeek is user-level (computed once); per-window projections are just different day-range sums of the same weekday averages.
  • Tab-aware labels: “If habits repeat, by tonight / by week end / by month end / at payday.”
  • Methodology modal gains a projections-per-window table showing all four scenarios side-by-side.
  • Deleted ExpandableBreakdownRow + ChildLine; replaced by the new DrillDownRow component.

User triggers

  • “It's hard to know that it can be clickable. The breakdown of the points of rows for what's in Credit Card Balance and what is Bills before payday is not great. Maybe we make this a small square box that can be scrollable to the left and right.”
  • “Shouldn't you include credit card transactions that are not included in the balance as of today? Things that you would predict as expenses in credit card until next payday, similar to how you have calculated for checking?”
  • “Shouldn't we bring this thinking into all of the tabs? Why just until payday?”

Lesson

Three tightly-related lessons in one release. (1) Conservative scope choices read as hedging. Depository-only was “safe” but invisibly cut sample size 10× and pretended CC spending wasn't real. Own the scope or explain why you scoped down. (2) When the same math conceptually applies across UI slices (tabs), compute it once at the user level and project per-slice — don't force per-slice repeated compute or per-slice feature gaps. (3) Tappable = chevron. Typography-only affordance (underline) was too subtle; iOS users look for the right-chevron.

v4.8
2026-04-23
EVOLVE
Scroll gesture fix + per-window-day cards + “you’d likely have” copy

What changed

  • Horizontal scroll fixed. v4.7 card strips wouldn’t swipe — the outer <Pressable onPress={toggleExpanded}> that wrapped the entire SafeToSpend card was swallowing ScrollView gestures. Restructured so only the hero header (amount + subtitle + chevron) lives inside the toggle Pressable; the drill-down section lives outside it.
  • Per-window-day cards. The Typical Spending drill-down now shows ONE CARD PER DAY in the selected tab’s window (not 7 weekday summaries). For Until Payday’s 9-day window, you see 9 cards: Thu Apr 23, Fri Apr 24, Sat Apr 25, Sun Apr 26, Mon Apr 27, Tue Apr 28, Wed Apr 29, Thu Apr 30, Fri May 1. Each card shows weekday name + date + that day’s typical spend. Users can now see exactly WHY the sum is what it is — in user 53’s case, Thursday and Friday appear twice in the Until Payday window, which is what drives the total past This Month.
  • Projection total label reworded. "If habits repeat, at payday" → "You’d likely have $X left at payday (May 1)". Names the outcome (remaining balance) instead of forcing the user to infer it. Per-tab variants: "by tonight", "by Sun Apr 27", "by Apr 30", "at payday (May 1)".

User triggers

  • “I’m not able to scroll through any of those square boxes to the left or right.”
  • “I don’t know how typical spending for Until Payday for 9 days is $2,658, whereas Today it’s $666 something.”
  • “If habits repeat by payday ... like you have another certain amount, like is that what’s remaining? I think we need to make that a little bit more clear.”

Lessons

  • Don’t wrap a scroll container inside a tap-Pressable. React Native’s gesture system gives the outer Pressable first dibs on touch; horizontal scroll gestures get interpreted as taps.
  • Show the addends, not the group’s averages — when a sum spans multiple instances of the same group (two Thursdays in one window), per-group summaries hide the explanation.
  • Projection copy should name the outcome, not the hypothesis. "If habits repeat" makes the user do the inference work; "You’d likely have $X left" just tells them.
PR #91 feat(safe-to-spend): v4.8 — scroll-gesture fix, per-window-day cards, projection copy clarity
v4.9
2026-04-25
CURRENT
Savings exclusion as a behavioral choice + open-question dump from on-device review

What changed (code)

  • Spendable balance filter changed from account_type = 'depository' to account_type = 'depository' AND account_subtype = 'checking'. Savings, money-market, HSA, and CD balances no longer count toward Safe to Spend.
  • New setAsideAccounts[] field on the API response — savings/HSA/MM/CD accounts surfaced separately for transparency (mirror of the existing loansExcluded[] pattern).
  • New “Set aside in savings” row in the SafeToSpend accordion (only renders when the user has any setAside accounts) — drill-down shows per-account balance with a subtitle of "savings, HSA, money market, etc."

Why this is the right call (deliberate behavioral choice)

Vik: "Yes, I think we need to help people save more. I would like the safe to spend component to reflect that in the math and UX. Yes, it's a deliberate choice, not a hacky label choice."

Including savings in “safe to spend” would silently encourage users to dip into their savings the moment runway feels tight. Excluding it surfaces saving as separate, protected money — the “set aside” framing reinforces that it’s not for spending. Same psychological pattern Apple, Wealthfront, and behavioral finance research consistently endorse.

What got documented (no code change)

Long thread of on-device review with Vik produced a list of follow-on questions, simplifications, and planned features. All captured in the math explorer’s expanded "Open Questions" section:

  • Plaid Liabilities-based CC due-date splitting — split the CC line into "Due [date]" + "Accumulating for next cycle" using statement balance + due date from Plaid Liabilities (we have production access). Same total subtracted, much better clarity.
  • Apple Card-style payment simulator — slider/wheel UX showing 25%/50%/75%/100% payment scenarios with interest impact.
  • Plaid Signal for live spend tracking — close the post-swipe latency gap (today: pending charges in minutes-to-hours, posted in 1-3 days).
  • Detector coverage gap — recurring stuff with thin history gets misclassified. User 53’s BILT rent ($4,200/mo) is the canonical example: 2 occurrences in 102 days, not detected as recurring → lands in typical-discretionary → inflates Thursday average from ~$15 to $666. Fix: parallel "Confirm a recurring bill" UX.
  • Lookback bump — 365 → 730 days when users approach 1 year of history.

What also got fixed in docs (no code, but real)

  • Typical-spending spec: clarified the misleading "zero-spend days are NOT in this map" wording (they ARE included as zeros in the per-weekday samples; just not in the intermediate Map).
  • Typical-spending spec: noted that excludeCreditCardSideOfCcPayment is defense-in-depth, not a primary inclusion path — CC payments are already excluded by other filters.
  • Math explorer: Spendable Balance card updated to reflect checking-only filter and savings exclusion rationale.
  • Typical-spending spec: new "Why is your Thursday so high?" section walking through the BILT story.

Lessons

  • Filter mismatches are silent until they aren’t. The label said "Checking" for weeks; the filter included savings. We just got lucky that user 53 had no savings accounts.
  • Behavioral defaults > "be neutral." Showing savings as part of safe-to-spend looks neutral but quietly nudges users toward worse outcomes. Excluding it is opinionated, but the right opinion.
  • Detector coverage gap is the single biggest distortion in typical-discretionary today. Real recurring stuff misclassified as discretionary inflates averages by orders of magnitude. Confirm-a-recurring-bill is the unblocking feature.
