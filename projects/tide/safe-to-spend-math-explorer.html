The end-to-end model — the daily ledger

L(d) = spendableBalance + Σ inflows(≤d) − Σ outflows(≤d)projected day-by-day, today → max(month end, payday−1), user’s timezone
↓ every tab is a read of this one curve
Until Payday = the FLOOR: min L(d), d ∈ [today, payday−1]the most you can spend right now and still cover everything before the next paycheck — the hero number
Today = dailyBudget = the sustainable RATE: min over d of L(d) / daysUntil(d)spending this rate never drives ANY pre-payday day below zero
This Week = min(rate × daysInWindow, floor)allocation view, capped — a shorter tab never exceeds a longer one pre-payday
This Month = same allocation when month-end ≤ payday−1; full-curve floor when the month crosses paydaypaychecks land on their dates — income is never flat-added before it arrives
↓
Shortfalls are NEVER clampednegative floor ships as first-class shortfall fields {isShortfall, shortfallAmount, earliestShortfallDate, bindingConstraint} — Home and Coach render it identically

Outflows are a MECE partition (bills/utils/subs · loans · planned savings · CC due-this-cycle · essentials); inflows are band-gated income occurrences. Everything recurring is occurrence-expanded: a 3-paycheck month counts 3 paychecks; a weekly bill counts every week. typicalDiscretionary stays context-only, never subtracted.

The components — click any card to drill in

+
Spendable Balance The ledger’s starting point — checking cash now

What it is

Sum of your active checking accounts only, using availableBalance when Plaid provides it (nets pending debits automatically) and falling back to currentBalance. This seeds the entire curve. Savings, money-market, HSA, and CD balances stay excluded and surface as setAsideAccounts[] — money you’ve moved to savings shouldn’t feel spendable.

Filters

  • account_type = 'depository' AND account_subtype = 'checking'
  • is_active = true

Load-bearing detail

Because availableBalance already nets pending debits, the ±4-day paid/pending dedupe (see Ledger Mechanics) must drop any projected occurrence matched to a pending transaction — otherwise the same bill hits the curve twice.

Bills / Utils / Subs Money Out 1 — recurring outflows, conf ≥ 0.3

What it is

Occurrence-expanded outflows for streams with effective category bill, utility, or subscription and confidence_score ≥ 0.3 (BILLS_MIN_CONFIDENCE). Each occurrence lands on its predicted date in the ledger; each window’s moneyOut.billsUtilsSubs sums what falls inside it.

Filters

  • effective category ∈ (bill, utility, subscription); legacy category NULL rows map via stream_type
  • is_active = true, confidence ≥ 0.3 (below → possibleBills[], NOT in the math)
  • not stale, occurrence not already paid/pending (see Ledger Mechanics)

Loans & Planned Savings Money Out 2+3 — obligations with their own labels

Loans (category loan)

Loan payment streams — car payment, student-loan autopay. Included regardless of confidence score (a detected loan payment is a real obligation); still subject to stale suppression and dedupe. Loan account balances stay out of the math entirely (loansExcluded[] — your mortgage principal isn’t due this month).

Planned savings (category planned_savings)

Recurring checking→savings transfers. They reduce Safe to Spend — the money does leave checking — but carry their own moneyOut.plannedSavings label, never lumped into “bills.” “You can’t spend this because you’re SAVING it” is a different story from “Comcast takes it.”

CC Due This Cycle Money Out 4 — statement net of payments, on the due date

What it is

Per card, the amount actually due this cycle, landing on the card’s due date in the ledger — never the total revolving balance (v4’s “acknowledged semantic weirdness,” now fixed). creditCardDebt (total owed) is still returned but is display-only; it is never subtracted anywhere in v5.

The per-card fallback ladder (ccObligations[].basis)

  • statement — max(0, last_statement_balance − payments since issue) on next_payment_due_date. Due date passed + overdue → due NOW (lands today); passed + not overdue → cycle settled → $0.
  • stream_estimate — no statement data: a detected checking→card autopay stream’s predictedNextAmount on its date, labeled “estimated.”
  • minimum — no stream either: minimum_payment_amount on the due date.
  • unknown — none of the above with a balance > 0 → $0 enters the math and ccDueUnknown = true so the UI says “we can’t see what’s due” instead of silently pretending.

Single-card payment-netting caveat

“Payments since issue” comes from checking debits matching LOAN_PAYMENTS_CREDIT_CARD% — which carry no per-card mapping. Netting therefore only applies with exactly ONE card; multi-card users get the raw statement per card (conservative).

ccNextCycleWarning

Per card, nextCycleAccruing = max(0, totalBalance − statement); the sum ships as ccNextCycleWarning {amount, monthLabel, escalate} — “$X building toward your next statement,” escalating to a Home banner when it exceeds 50% of the expected paycheck (CC_WARNING_PAYCHECK_RATIO). Server-computed so the Coach narrates the identical rule.

Essentials Forecast Money Out 5 — necessary daily spend, whitelist-scoped

What it is

Projected daily NECESSARY non-recurring spend, spread per-weekday across every ledger day — the term that stops the hero from saying “spend $200” when $90 of it is groceries you will definitely buy.

Deliberately narrower than typical-discretionary

  • Category whitelist (ESSENTIALS_PFC_PREFIXES, v1): groceries, gas, public transit, pharmacy, primary care, childcare. Subtracting ALL typical spend would make the hero circular.
  • Checking/debit-funded only: card-swiped groceries exit checking when the STATEMENT is paid — they’re already in the CC-due term. Counting them here too is the double-count.

Methodology (essentials_whitelist_robust_mean_dow_p90)

  • Per-weekday robust mean, p90-capped; zero-spend days included (“typical daily essentials,” not “how much when you shop”)
  • A weekday needs ≥ 4 observations or contributes $0
  • Cold start: < 14 observed days → term omitted (essentials.active = false, hero labeled “still learning”)
  • Blend phase-in: blendFactor = min(1, lookbackDays / 60) — no day-60 cliff where the hero suddenly drops by hundreds overnight

Expected Income Band-gated occurrences with floor semantics

What it is

Income stream occurrences, with the amount admitted per the app-wide confidence taxonomy (confidence_bands.ts:confidenceBand — a user declaration is ALWAYS high):

  • high (confirmed/declared or score ≥ 0.85) → full predicted amount; the declared Settings payday_amount substitutes on the confirmed stream (amountBasis: 'declared')
  • medium (0.5–0.85) → p25 of observed deposits (“estimated”) — a $700–$1,300 gig earner gets ~$800, not $0 and not the optimistic mean
  • low (< 0.5) → excluded entirely; transfer_in never counts

Floor semantics — the v5 income rule

Income is never flat-added to a window. Each paycheck occurrence lands on its date, lifting L(d) only for days at/after arrival — a paycheck 5 days out cannot rescue rent due in 2 days (see the shortfall example). Occurrence expansion applies: a 3-paycheck month counts all 3.

Ledger Mechanics Expansion · stale suppression · ±4-day dedupe

1. Occurrence expansion

Every included stream is expanded over [today, range end] via recurrence_detection_service.ts:expandOccurrences (period/anchor-day aware). v4 counted only the single predictedNextDate; v5 counts every occurrence in the range — weekly bills count every week.

2. Stale suppression

A stream whose predictedNextDate is > 0.75× its detected period overdue with no matching transaction is treated as likely-cancelled: demoted to possibleBills[], out of the math (isStreamStale). Applies to all outflow categories, including loans and planned savings.

3. Paid/pending dedupe (±4 days)

A projected occurrence is DROPPED when a member transaction (posted OR pending) exists within DEDUPE_TOLERANCE_DAYS = 4 days of it — the balance already reflects it (availableBalance nets pending debits). Applies to outflows AND income.

The Floor & The Rate deriveLedgerTabs — how the four tabs read the curve

Until Payday — the floor (hero)

min L(d), d ∈ [today, payday−1]. The window ends payday−1: the awaited paycheck is never inside its own runway; the number jumps when the deposit actually posts.

Today — dailyBudget, the sustainable rate

x = min over d of L(d)/daysUntil(d). Spending x/day never drives ANY pre-payday day below zero. Deliberately NOT floor ÷ daysUntilPayday and NOT floor-day balance ÷ days-to-floor-day — see the worked example for why $25/day is right where $66/day digs −$300.

This Week / This Month

Week: min(x × daysInWindow, floor) — a shorter tab never exceeds a longer one pre-payday. Month (field still named thisMonth until the mobile v5 PR): same allocation when month-end ≤ payday−1; when the month crosses payday, the TRUE full-curve floor through month end — paychecks land on their dates.

Shortfall — never clamped

Negative floor → allocations collapse to the floor (a negative rate × more days would read “deeper in the hole the longer you wait”) and the response carries shortfall {isShortfall, shortfallAmount, earliestShortfallDate, bindingConstraint {date, label}}. Home renders it; the Coach quotes the identical projection. v4 clamped negatives to $0 in the Coach; v5 never clamps anywhere.

Typical Discretionary CONTEXT — still never subtracted

What it is

Forecast of ALL non-recurring spend (checking AND credit card) between today and each tab’s windowEnd — robust per-weekday mean, p90-capped, up to a year of lookback. Surfaced as the “you’d likely have $X left” projection line.

Don’t confuse it with essentials

Essentials is the narrower whitelist term (necessary categories only, checking-funded only) that DOES enter the ledger. Typical discretionary is the full projection that does NOT — subtracting it would make the hero circular (“safe to spend” minus the discretionary you’ll spend anyway).

How the four tabs read the same curve

One ledger, four reads. The curve L(d) is tab-invariant; what changes is the window end and whether the tab is a floor, a rate, or a capped allocation.

Worked example 1 — why the rate is $25/day, not $66/day

$1,000 in checking. $800 rent due day 3. Payday day 9 → pre-payday window is days 1–8. No other events.

Day d:      1      2      3      4      5      6      7      8    | 9 (payday)
Event:      —      —    −800     —      —      —      —      —    | +paycheck
L(d):     1000   1000    200    200    200    200    200    200   |
L(d)/d:   1000    500   66.67    50     40   33.33  28.57  25.00  |

Until Payday (floor)    = min L(d)         = $200
Today (rate)            = min L(d)/d = 200/8 = $25.00/day
This Week (ends day 7)  = min(25 × 7, 200)  = $175
This Month (ends day 8) = min(25 × 8, 200)  = $200

Why NOT $66/day: $66.67 is 200/3 — it merely clears the rent-day dip. Keep spending $66.67/day through day 8 and you’ve spent ~$533 against $200 of post-rent headroom: 1000 − 800 − 533 ≈ −$333 by payday. The min runs over every pre-payday day, so the binding ratio is 200/8 and $25/day keeps every day ≥ $0.

If the month crossed payday (month-end day 20, $3,000 paycheck day 9), This Month becomes the full-curve floor over days 1–20 — the paycheck lifts L(d) only from day 9 onward, so the floor stays $200. Income is never flat-added into earlier days.

Worked example 2 — shortfall: income can’t mask an earlier dip

$500 in checking. $1,000 rent due day 2. $2,000 paycheck lands day 5 (payday) → pre-payday window is days 1–4.

Day d:      1      2      3      4    | 5 (payday)
Event:      —    −1000    —      —    | +2000
L(d):      500   −500   −500   −500   | +1500
L(d)/d:    500   −250  −166.67 −125   |

Until Payday (floor) = −$500   ← NOT clamped
Today (rate)         = −$250
This Week / Month    = floor (allocation collapses in shortfall) = −$500

shortfall = { isShortfall: true, shortfallAmount: −500,
              earliestShortfallDate: day 2,
              bindingConstraint: { date: day 2, label: "rent" } }

The $2,000 paycheck makes the post-payday curve healthy (+$1,500) but cannot mask the day-2 dip — floor semantics means income only helps days at/after it arrives. v4 flat-added window income and the Coach clamped negatives to $0; v5 tells the truth: you are $500 short, starting day 2, because of rent.

Open questions / known simplifications

  • Multi-card payment netting. Checking→card payment transactions (LOAN_PAYMENTS_CREDIT_CARD%) carry no per-card mapping, so statement netting only applies to single-card users; multi-card users get the conservative raw statement per card. Needs per-card payment attribution.
  • Detector coverage gap on recurring streams. Real recurring stuff with <3 occurrences gets misclassified as discretionary (User 53’s BILT rent is the canonical example). Fix: “Confirm a recurring bill” UX (E2.1) — user_confirmed=true bypasses the occurrence-count threshold.
  • Essentials whitelist is v1. ESSENTIALS_PFC_PREFIXES covers groceries, fuel/transit, pharmacy, primary care, childcare. Extend deliberately — in the spec and the code together.
  • Today’s essentials don’t net today’s completed swipes. Fresh-day estimate; could be tightened to “remaining today.”
  • Lookback bump from 365 → 730 days. Plaid gives 2 years; we cap at 365 conservatively. Bump once users approach 1y of history (E2.2).
  • Plaid Signal for live spend pressure. The swipe-to-ledger latency gap (bank → Plaid → us → mobile) remains; Signal targets near-real-time spend tracking (E3.1).
  • Savings deliberately excluded from spendable balance. Kept from v4.9 — surfaced as setAsideAccounts[] (“Set aside in savings”). Encourages users to keep savings savings.
  • Loan accounts excluded. Loan payments are in the math (category loan); loan balances surface as loansExcluded[] only.
